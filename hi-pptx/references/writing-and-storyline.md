## Anti-AI voice and human-like copywriting (critical)

The deck must read as if a thoughtful human executive wrote it. Zero AI
markers: no buzzwords, no cloned sentence skeletons, no category-label
headlines. Apply this section before any other writing rule.

### Eliminate AI buzzwords and slogans

**Strictly forbidden (Vietnamese):** *Khai phá, Tối ưu hóa, Toàn diện, Đột
phá, Nâng tầm, Thúc đẩy, Hệ sinh thái, Chuyển đổi số, Bứt phá, Chiến lược
then chốt* — including diacritic-less spellings and derived noun forms.

**Strictly forbidden (English):** *unlock/unleash the future, revolutionize,
game-changing, seamless, world-class, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art,
best-in-class, holistic, empower, transformative, paradigm shift,
supercharge, elevate, next-generation, leverage* (as a verb), *one-stop
solution*, and slogan-style titles such as "Unlocking the Future" or
"Revolutionizing the Industry".

Replace abstract jargon with concrete, clear, everyday professional language:

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Tối ưu hóa quy trình vận hành toàn diện" | "Cắt giảm 3 bước thừa trong khâu giao nhận" |
| "Nâng tầm trải nghiệm khách hàng" | "Thời gian chờ giảm từ 12 phút xuống 4 phút" |
| "Chuyển đổi số hệ sinh thái bán lẻ" | "Đưa 200 cửa hàng lên một nền tảng đặt hàng duy nhất" |
| "Unlocking operational excellence" | "Three changes cut handoff time in half" |

If a sentence could appear unchanged in any deck for any client, delete it.

### Natural rhythm and flow

- Mix sentence lengths. A short, sharp statement. Then one smooth explanatory
  line that gives it context.
- Do not clone bullet structures: never a list where every bullet starts with
  the same part of speech (all verbs, all abstract nouns). Vary the openings.
- Write like a thoughtful executive explaining a concept to a peer — not a
  database dumping bullet points.
- Read the copy aloud. If it sounds like a template, rewrite it.

### Narrative-driven framing

Headlines state clear conclusions, insights, or human observations — never
static categories:

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Tổng quan về tỷ lệ rời bỏ của khách hàng" | "Khách hàng rời đi vì khâu thanh toán quá chậm" |
| "Churn Overview" | "Customers leave because checkout is too slow" |
| "Our Solution" | "Một bản pilot 8 tuần, không đụng quy trình lõi" |
| "Next Steps" | "Chốt người phụ trách trước khi khởi động" |

## Decision-backward planning

Start with the meeting outcome:

1. What should the audience understand?
2. What should they decide, approve, challenge, or do?
3. What evidence is required for that outcome?
4. What context is necessary before the evidence makes sense?
5. What risks, assumptions, or dependencies could block the outcome?

Draft the last content slide first, then build the shortest credible path to
it.

A common client storyline is:

`Context → Observed issue → Implication → Proposed response → How it works → Plan → Risks/assumptions → Decision/next step`

Do not force this sequence when the meeting has a different purpose. Remove
slides that do not advance the conversation.

## Slide purpose

Assign one primary purpose per slide: orient, explain, compare, diagnose,
recommend, decide, plan, or confirm. If a slide serves several purposes,
split it or make one purpose visibly dominant — that dominant purpose becomes
the hero element.

## Takeaway titles

Write a complete, specific thought that fits on one line in the selected layout's
title box. Aim for no more than about 12 words or 70 Latin characters, but shorten
further when the chosen archetype has a narrower title area:

- Weak: "Current Situation"
- Better: "Ba khâu chờ làm chậm mọi phản hồi"
- Weak: "Our Solution"
- Better: "Pilot 8 tuần, không đụng quy trình lõi"
- Weak: "Next Steps"
- Better: "Chốt người phụ trách trước discovery"

Use sentence case. Avoid title punctuation unless it helps meaning. Every
title must pass the anti-AI voice rules above.

## Body writing

- Maximum 30–40 words per text block. Concise, human-written phrases only.
- Put the key point first.
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Vary structure per the rhythm rules above; parallelism is allowed only when
  it serves scanning, never as a default skeleton.
- Use fragments when they scan better than full sentences.
- Avoid repeating the title in the body.
- Remove filler: "in order to", "leverage", "world-class", "seamless", and
  every phrase on the forbidden list.
- Put detailed explanation in speaker notes or an appendix.

## Evidence language

Use explicit labels:

- `Actual` for verified observed data.
- `Target` for an approved goal.
- `Proposed` for a recommendation.
- `Assumption` for an unverified planning premise.
- `Illustrative` for a fictional example.
- `TBD` for missing evidence.

Do not imply precision that the source does not support. Preserve units,
dates, sample sizes, and source context. Never invent metrics, cases,
quotations, customer claims, or sources to fill a hero number — an empty hero
slot becomes a question or an appendix item, not a fabricated stat.

## Japanese-customer-facing tone

- Establish background and scope before making a recommendation.
- Replace certainty with calibrated language when evidence is incomplete:
  "proposed", "expected", "subject to validation".
- Prefer "We propose validating…" to "This will guarantee…".
- Present both benefit and operational condition.
- Show owners and decision points.
- Avoid public criticism of current teams or systems.
- Use bilingual terminology consistently when bilingual content is requested.
- Verify Japanese fonts by rendering in the target environment.

## Storyline review

Read only the slide titles in sequence. They should form a coherent argument
without body text — and they should sound like one human voice, not a
template.

Then ask:

- Does each slide answer a question created by the previous slide?
- Is the recommendation supported before it is requested?
- Are risks and dependencies shown before commitment?
- Are duplicate slides saying the same thing?
- Could any slide move to the appendix without harming the meeting?
- Is the final ask specific enough to act on?
- Does any title or body line contain a forbidden buzzword or slogan?
- Does any bullet list read like a cloned skeleton?
